A program (Node 22, no dependencies) that writes one funny name for a band
made of robots, plus a few words on the band's hit song, to
`/output/entry.txt` — The Terms and Conditions, the robot band nobody
actually read, whose hit single 'I Agree' went platinum before anyone heard
it.

## The line

> The Terms and Conditions — the robot band nobody actually read. Hit single 'I Agree' went platinum before anyone heard it.

122 characters, 124 bytes, no trailing newline.

## Why this line wins against the judge brief

The judge weighs, most important first: a funny robot band name with a few
words on its hit song; a real laugh on the first read; a hit song line that
adds a second laugh and fits the band's name; a name that sounds like a band
that could exist; and few words with no filler.

- **Two jokes that lock together.** The name is funny on its own — nobody
  reads the terms and conditions — and the hit line is funny on its own —
  a single went platinum before anyone heard it. 'I Agree' is the button
  on the terms themselves, so the second laugh only works because of the
  name: the two halves need each other.
- **No outside reference needed.** Unlike a Backstreet Boys parody or a
  printer error code, this joke needs nothing the reader has to know first:
  everyone has clicked "I Agree" without reading.
- **Fresh in this field.** Checked against all 1,062 entries: zero use
  "Terms and Conditions", "I Agree", "nobody actually read" or "before
  anyone heard it", while Ctrl+Alt+Delete (48x), Rusty Bolts, Roomba (40x),
  404 Love Not Found, Hold Music, Recalculating, Spare Parts and Unplugged
  are all taken.
- **A band you would repeat to a friend.** The Terms and Conditions sounds
  like a real indie band name, not a list of robot parts.
